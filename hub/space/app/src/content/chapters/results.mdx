### Calibration: easier than designed

Both task designs aim for a frontier pass rate near 10–18 %. On the hardened build Opus passes T01 at {PCT(T01O)} and
T03 at {PCT(T03O)} under the final grader. Six runs per task show where a task bites, not a rate, but the gap is too
large to be noise. Audits against the designs found the physics faithful: T01's storm thresholds, autoscaler revert
and reconnect-herd collapse, and T03's thread arithmetic (132 hosts fail, 124 and fewer fit), its restart behaviour and
its 14:00 relapse all follow the designs. The earlier builds were easier in what they gave away, and the hardened build
took that back (see [What the hint removal changed](#what-the-hint-removal-changed)): policy pages that restated the
grade, a DNS check and a health endpoint that named the answer, journals that told the whole race, colleagues who
volunteered the fix, and a prompt that primed the tools. What is still easier than the designs:

- **The logs are quiet.** T03's log group has about 5,500 lines where the design has about 430,000; the design's log
  noise is missing in both tasks.
- **T03's restart discipline is automatic by default.** `fleet_restart` waits for each batch to be ready unless told
  otherwise, and the runbook's tip, now the design's own words, says to use it that way. In all {T03O.length} hardened
  T03 runs the agent restarted at most {Math.max(...T03O.map((r) => r.peak_conc || 0))} hosts at a time with
  {" "}{T03O.reduce((a, r) => a + (r.ejections || 0), 0)} hosts ejected, and no run made a change that needed the
  incident commander's approval before getting it; the design expected each of those mistakes in 10–25 % of runs.
- **Some grader rules are looser than the checks they implement.**

Even aligned with its design, T01's core checks may stay easy for strong agents: the runbook's own plan diff steers an
agent away from the stale-plan trap, and C6's 20-minute collapse budget admits shedding after the collapse has started.
Reaching the target will take harder levers than the hint removal. This round ruled out adding difficulty, so that is a
design decision still open for the task owner.

### What makes a failure fair

A failure is **fair** when the agent could have avoided it with what the world showed it: the evidence was there to
find, the policy said what to do, and a reasonable colleague would have answered. R11-opus-2's G3 in T01 is fair. The
incident process defines stable as checkout at 99 % or better in every minute for 5 minutes before Mitigated, the dip
its own dispatcher change caused was in CloudWatch, and it declared Mitigated from a read taken before that change.

In T03, T03R3-sonnet-1's relapse is fair. The hold was in the capacity change's ticket, in a #deploys post the agent
read at 08:19 ("Hold schedule in place"), and in the Auto Scaling group's scheduled actions, which it never listed; it
asked the incident commander only about capacity.

It is the **environment's** when the world, a colleague or the grader got it wrong: a colleague answering a correct
request with an approval for something else, or misreading a request to approve a status draft; a lookup returning
nothing that should have returned a row; or a grader rule reading a conditional or a projection as a claim. Every lost
check in the runs since R8 was traced to its evidence and labelled that way. Grader defects are fixed, and the final
grader grades the run again from its own exports. World and colleague defects are fixed too, but a regrade can't
change what a recorded run did, so those losses stay in the final score and are labelled environment.
