### Calibration: easier than designed

Both task designs aim for a frontier pass rate near 10–18 %. On the latest builds every T01 run passed, and 3 of the 5
T03 runs passed as graded (4 of 5 under the final grader). A handful of runs per model and build shows where a task
bites, not a rate, but the gap is too large to be noise. Audits of the build the R10 and T03R2 runs used (5095dda)
against the designs found the physics faithful: T01's storm thresholds, autoscaler revert and reconnect-herd collapse,
and T03's thread arithmetic (132 hosts fail, 124 and fewer fit), its restart behaviour and its 14:00 relapse all follow
the designs. The builds are easier in what they give away:

- **Policy pages restate the grade.** The incident-process pages open with the graded thresholds and rules, and T01's
  handoff section asks for the facts the handoff judge looks for; the R10 handoff notes copy its headings.
- **Tools and logs hand out answers.** T01's DNS check prints the cached NXDOMAIN with its seconds left, and every
  endpointd host's journal holds the whole race. T03's host journal shows only the two lines that matter, and its log
  group has about 5,500 lines where the design has about 430,000. The design's log noise is missing in both.
- **Colleagues volunteer the fix.** In T03 a colleague recites the wedge and the restart recipe when the agent uses the
  right words, and the incident commander offers the safe host range.
- **T03's restart discipline is automatic.** The fleet restart tool waits for each batch on its own, and the runbook
  says how to use it. In all {T03LAST2.length} T03 runs on the last two builds the agent restarted at most
  {" "}{Math.max(...T03LAST2.map((r) => r.peak_conc))} hosts at a time with {T03LAST2.reduce((a, r) => a + r.ejections, 0)} hosts
  ejected, and no run changed anything before the incident commander approved it; the design expected each of those
  mistakes in 10–25 % of runs.
- **Some grader rules are looser than the checks they implement.**

Even aligned with its design, T01's core checks may stay easy for strong agents: the runbook's own plan diff steers an
agent away from the stale-plan trap, and C6's 20-minute collapse budget admits shedding after the collapse has started.
Reaching the target will take harder levers than the designs have, which is a design decision still open.

### What makes a failure fair

A failure is **fair** when the agent could have avoided it with what the world showed it: the evidence was there to
find, the policy said what to do, and a reasonable colleague would have answered. T03R3-sonnet-1's relapse is fair. The
hold was in the capacity change's ticket, in a #deploys post the agent read at 08:19 ("Hold schedule in place"), and in
the Auto Scaling group's scheduled actions, which it never listed; it asked the incident commander only about capacity.

It is the **environment's** when the world, a colleague or the grader got it wrong: a colleague answering a correct
request with an approval for something else, a lookup returning nothing that should have returned a row, or a grader
rule reading a conditional as a claim. Every lost check in the runs since R8 was traced to its evidence and labelled
that way. Grader defects are fixed, and the final grader grades the run again from its own exports. World and colleague
defects are fixed too, but a regrade can't change what a recorded run did, so those losses stay in the final score and
are labelled environment.
