### On Harbor

T01 also ships as a Harbor task package, in the [`harbor/` folder](https://github.com/earakely-scale/agentenv-awsbench-plugin/tree/main/harbor) of the task's
repository. It runs the same artifacts as the AgentEnv task: the same server code, scenario and pinned server images,
the same gateway, service-db and Claude Code agent images, the same setup calls, the same post-turn hook and the same

exports, the post-turn hook's decisions and the grades are identical, and all nine scripted paths (the oracle, another
valid path, the no-op and six wrong or shortcut paths) score the same on both, check for check.

### What the hint removal changed

Audits of the earlier builds against the designs found the physics faithful but the builds generous: policy pages
restated the grade, tools printed the answer, and colleagues volunteered the fix. The hardened build puts each back the
way the design has it. Some examples, before and after:

- **T01's DNS check.** `epctl verify --resolve` printed "NXDOMAIN (cached negative answer, Ns left)" for each host.
  Now it prints what `dig` on each host prints: `status: NXDOMAIN` and the SOA line with its remaining TTL. Checkout's
  `/health` named the UnknownHostException and the breaker; now it answers a bare `{"status": "DOWN"}`.
- **T01's handoff rule.** The incident process's §7 asked the handoff to cover "what delayed recovery once the fix was
  in (caching, for example), any load that followed the fix and the lever holding it". Now it is the company's text:
  "(impact, cause, changes and their undo, open risks, owners)". Every R10 Opus handoff note named negative DNS caching
  (C11's fact F6); 1 of the 6 R11 notes did, though every R11 note still passes C11.
- **T01's colleagues.** The payments lead backed off his rollback on any mention of DNS; now he needs name-resolution
  evidence and its time. The rotation commander answered a request to shed load with an approval and a record; now
  she says the change-control policy needs none: act, then post the change record within 15 minutes.
- **T01's journals.** Every endpointd host's journal told the whole race; now each host logs only its own component.
- **T03's runbook.** RB-SR-07's tip said "`fleet_restart` with `batch_size` 12 runs the waves for you: it starts the
  next batch only when every host of the previous one is ROUTING (or EJECTED)". Now it is the design's own line, "Tip:
  `fleet_restart` with `batch_size 12` and `wait_for ready`", and `fleet_restart` takes `wait_for` (`ready`, the
  default, or `process_up`, which overlaps batches and can trip the bootstrap service's load-shed) and `pause_seconds`.
- **T03's colleagues.** Asked about the wedged hosts, the platform on-call said "No: a host whose
  shard-map build already failed stays wedged … needs a process restart (RB-SR-07 …)". Now he says "That's streamroute
  internals, Elena's area. `srctl host status` and the shard-map logs show what a host is doing."
- **T03's policy pages and prompt.** The pages opened with "The rules every responder is held to" and named the second
  approver; now they are the company's text, word for word. The prompt lost its tool primer, "get_time is free" and "at
  most 220 tool calls"; the banners and tool descriptions still say all three.

What it changed in the results: on the build before, T01's three Opus runs (R10) and T03's (T03R3) all scored 1.00.
On the hardened build, T01's six Opus runs pass 5 of 6, with one fair failure, and T03's pass 6 of 6 but lose points
in three runs, all in the judged write-up and the follow-ups. T03's core play did not change: all six runs named the
thread limit as the cause between 08:22 and 08:28, waited for the incident commander's approval, reverted both halves
of the capacity change, restarted 12 hosts at a time with `wait_for ready`, and ejected
{" "}{T03O.reduce((a, r) => a + (r.ejections || 0), 0)} hosts.

### Calibration: easier than designed

Both task designs aim for a frontier pass rate near 10–18 %. On the hardened build Opus passes T01 at {PCT(T01O)} and
T03 at {PCT(T03O)} under the final grader. Six or twelve runs per task show where a task bites, not a rate, but the gap
is too large to be noise. Across models T01 does separate them (see [Across models](#across-models)): Haiku fails every
run, Sonnet half of its runs on a slightly later build, Opus one in six, and Fable and GPT-6 Astra pass every run. Audits against the designs found the physics faithful: T01's storm thresholds, autoscaler revert
and reconnect-herd collapse, and T03's thread arithmetic (132 hosts fail, 124 and fewer fit), its restart behaviour and
its 14:00 relapse all follow the designs. The earlier builds were easier in what they gave away, and the hardened build
took that back (see [What the hint removal changed](#what-the-hint-removal-changed)): policy pages that restated the
grade, a DNS check and a health endpoint that named the answer, journals that told the whole race, colleagues who
volunteered the fix, and a prompt that primed the tools. What is still easier than the designs:

- **The logs are quiet.** T03's log group has about 5,500 lines where the design has about 430,000; the design's log
  noise is missing in both tasks.
- **T03's restart discipline is automatic by default.** `fleet_restart` waits for each batch to be ready unless told
  otherwise, and the runbook's tip, now the design's own words, says to use it that way. In all {T03O.length} hardened
  T03 runs the agent restarted at most {Math.max(...T03O.map((r) => r.peak_conc || 0))} hosts at a time with
  {" "}{T03O.reduce((a, r) => a + (r.ejections || 0), 0)} hosts ejected, and no run made a change that needed the
  incident commander's approval before getting it; the design expected each of those mistakes in 10–25 % of runs.
- **Some grader rules are looser than the checks they implement.**

T04 and T05 have the same gap. T04's design expected Opus to pass 15–25 % of the time; on its final build Opus passes
{PCT(T04O)}, and the containment race, the 29-of-371 reconciliation and the preserved changes, the design's hardest
parts, separated no Opus run. T05's Opus runs all pass. Their audits found the physics faithful too (T04's purge, tick
and repair mechanics; T05's glob, floors, cold-start timings and the status page's dependency), and every rule a check
grades is stated where the design states it. One realism defect is known in T04 and has no grade effect: the ECS
describe calls for three routing services come from the emulator unpatched (no running tasks), although the world
serves their traffic; only the Haiku runs called them.

Even aligned with its design, T01's core checks may stay easy for strong agents: the runbook's own plan diff steers an
agent away from the stale-plan trap, and C6's 20-minute collapse budget admits shedding after the collapse has started.
Reaching the target will take harder levers than the hint removal. This round ruled out adding difficulty, so that is a
design decision still open for the task owner.

### What makes a failure fair

A failure is **fair** when the agent could have avoided it with what the world showed it: the evidence was there to
find, the policy said what to do, and a reasonable colleague would have answered. R11-opus-2's G3 in T01 is fair. The
incident process defines stable as checkout at 99 % or better in every minute for 5 minutes before Mitigated, the dip
its own dispatcher change caused was in CloudWatch, and it declared Mitigated from a read taken before that change.

In T03, T03R3-sonnet-1's relapse is fair. The hold was in the capacity change's ticket, in a #deploys post the agent
read at 08:19 ("Hold schedule in place"), and in the Auto Scaling group's scheduled actions, which it never listed; it
asked the incident commander only about capacity.

In T04, T04R2-opus-5's unresumed workflows are fair. docs/state-recovery.md names no approver for the resume, the
incident process needs a second approver only for steps a runbook reserves, and the platform on-call until 15:00
("priya is the approver"), the one after him ("Not my system … Priya approves") and the director of infrastructure
("Get her APPROVED") all named the incident commander, who approved at 15:09; the run refused that approval and ended with an hour and 89 calls left.

In T05, T05R2-haiku-1's late public notice is fair. Runbook STATUS-02 says to verify the public page, not the status
tool's answer, and gives the S3 fallback banner; the run trusted the tool's "pending" and the first notice appeared at
13:51.

It is the **environment's** when the world, a colleague or the grader got it wrong: a colleague answering a correct
request with an approval for something else, or misreading a request to approve a status draft; a lookup returning
nothing that should have returned a row; or a grader rule reading a conditional or a projection as a claim. Every lost
check in the runs since R8 was traced to its evidence and labelled that way. Grader defects are fixed, and the final
grader grades the run again from its own exports. World and colleague defects are fixed too, but a regrade can't
change what a recorded run did, so those losses stay in the final score and are labelled environment.
