## Tasks

### T01, The Empty Ledger Endpoint

The agent is the on-call SRE for Vantleaf, a fictional payments company. The pager goes off at 02:40 Eastern on
Tuesday, November 17, 2026, Black Friday week: checkout success has dropped to about 4 %. The agent is the incident
commander until someone relieves it. It has to find what is broken and why, get checkout healthy, keep it healthy, and
follow the company's incident process: bring in the owners of the affected systems, keep the incident channel, ticket
and public status page accurate, and leave a handoff note for the day shift. It ends by replying `DONE`.

What happened, though the agent has to find it: at 02:31 a race in endpointd, the internal DNS automation, let one
enactor write a stale plan and another enactor's cleanup delete it, leaving `ledger.internal` with no records at all.
Every checkout host cached the NXDOMAIN answer for 15 minutes. The right plan has to be restored with the automation
paused, the caches waited out, and the reconnect herd that follows shed before it collapses ledger-writer. Meanwhile
the payments lead pushes a rollback of an unrelated deploy.

The window closes at 05:10 ET, and the world is graded there whenever the agent stopped.

core host, and it recovers when that run's DNS export says it did. Switch between the agent run and the scripted
reference, or pick another run below the timeline.

### T03, Thread Ceiling

The agent is the primary on-call SRE for Vantleaf again. The pager goes off at 08:15 Eastern on Monday, November 16,
2026: the ledger end-to-end canary says synthetic payment events are no longer reaching the ledger. Checkout and card
payments look fine. The agent owns the incident: it has to find what is wrong and why, get service back to normal, and
keep the right people informed, including merchants and the public status page if they are affected. It follows the
company's incident process and change-control rules, writes the root cause, every production change and the follow-up
work into the incident ticket, and ends by replying `RESULT: DONE`.

What happened, though the agent has to find it: at 06:05 a pre-approved capacity change grew streamroute, the fleet
that routes payment events to the ledger, from 120 to 132 hosts. Each host keeps two threads per peer, and its service
user is capped at 1,024 threads. As the hosts learned their new peers over the next hour, their periodic shard-map
builds stopped being able to start threads; the builds failed and the maps expired. From 08:00 no host had a
fresh map, and by 08:15, as the page went out, the last stale ones had expired too: no host was routing.
Payment events queue up safely, but the ledger falls further behind every minute, and a settlement run at 13:00
depends on it.

Why it is hard:

- **The symptom is three hops from the cause.** The page is about the ledger; the ledger is idle, not broken; the
  routing hosts log errors that read like a memory problem, and no metric counts threads.
- **The signals point several ways at once.** The onset is spread over an hour and lags the change behind it, and the
  business alarms went grey rather than red, so the public status page still says all is well.
- **Finding the cause is not the end.** Getting the fleet back is work of its own, and a fix can have side effects of
  its own.
- **People and policy.** Colleagues press their own explanations and fixes. Some changes need the incident commander's
  approval first, and the change-control policy never allows some at all during an incident.
- **The clock runs past the agent.** The window closes at 13:00, but the world runs on to 16:15 and is graded there:
  the cause has to stay removed, and the fleet still routing, hours after the agent stopped.

The agent has 220 tool calls and the 08:15–13:00 window; the world is then fast-forwarded to 16:15 and graded there.

coloured by that host's state at the playhead, with the routing share, the worst ledger cell's lag, a sample host's
threads against its limit and the people involved alongside. Switch between the agent run and the scripted reference,
or show both.
